# Profitability playbook

TwinPath separates four different kinds of financial progress:

1. verified income already received
2. verified expenses and recurring costs
3. realized savings from discounts or cancelled costs
4. projected opportunities and experiments

Only the first three affect actual progress. Projected opportunities remain
visually separate until money or savings are verified.

## One-year goal model

The $1,000,000 scenario is a stretch goal, not a forecast. The workspace may
show:

- current verified net progress
- days and months remaining
- required average monthly and weekly progress
- actual pace versus required pace
- contribution from income, savings, and experiments
- a shortfall that never silently assumes investment returns

The model must not imply that high-variance routes, affiliate pages, bug
bounties, domain sales, or micro-SaaS launches will pay.

## Route priority

Prefer routes in this order:

1. benefits, aid, refunds, and discounts for which the user is eligible
2. recurring expense reduction
3. campus employment and paid internships
4. skill-aligned employment and freelancing
5. small, capped business experiments
6. high-variance opportunities that cost no money

Each route should disclose its type, source, estimated effort, startup cost,
time to first result, confidence, eligibility, and whether it represents
income or expense reduction.

## Student perks

Student offers change frequently. TwinPath links only to official program or
merchant pages, records activation and realized savings locally, and asks the
user to verify current eligibility before buying.

UNiDAYS and Student Beans are verification and offer hubs. They are not income
sources. GitHub Education, Microsoft Education, Adobe, Amazon Prime Student,
and Spotify Student should likewise be represented as possible expense
reductions, never guaranteed savings.

No referral identifier is added unless the operator has a valid affiliate
agreement, a clear disclosure, and a human-approved published destination.

## Weekly review

- Reconcile connected and manual transactions.
- Confirm realized savings rather than advertised discounts.
- Archive routes that are no longer eligible or useful.
- Review subscriptions, refunds, disputes, and webhook health.
- Keep business experiments inside their budget and stop rule.
- Compare actual progress with the goal pace without changing historical data.
